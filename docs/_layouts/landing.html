How it works

Three moves. Nobody leaves the page.

  1. 1

    Point at the element

    Hover the page, or use the arrow keys and Enter. No need to describe where the problem is.

  2. 2

    Write a note, send it

    It becomes an issue with what a developer needs, and a picture of the element when the site turns that on.

  3. 3

    Follow it up on the page

    Each pin takes the colour of its issue's status. Replies from your team show under the note.

  • NewJust sent
  • To doAccepted by the team
  • In progressBeing fixed
  • DoneFixed, ready to check
  • ClosedNot kept

The five stages and their colours. Each site can rename its statuses, recolour them, or add its own on top of these stages.

The three modes behind these cases

Connectors

Your tracker stays the source of truth

Connect a tracker once for the whole workspace. Each site picks where its notes go. The status of a Linear or GitHub issue is read back each time the page loads.

  • L LinearIssues in the team you pick
  • G GitHub IssuesIssues in a repository, through a GitHub App
  • { } Your own endpointEach note posted to your HTTPS address, signed
  • DB Your workspaceNo tracker: notes kept in its PostgreSQL

Jira, GitLab, Trello and Notion are not available yet.

Statuses, set per site

Every tracker names its states its own way. Each one lands on one of five stages, and a site can rename, recolour or re-stage them. Linear, by default:

  • TriageNew
  • TodoTo do
  • In ProgressIn progress
  • DoneDone
  • CanceledClosed

Questions

Do my clients need an account?

Not on a public site: a visitor leaves a note with no account, and can add a name. On a site only your team reads, invite the client as a guest: they sign in with a link sent by email.

What if I cannot add a script to the client's site?

Use the browser extension, for Chrome and Firefox. It adds Fruitback for the people who switch it on, and nothing changes on the site. It is in beta, on the Chrome Web Store for Chrome and Edge, and on addons.mozilla.org for Firefox. In another browser you load it by hand, as the reviewer's guide shows.

Which trackers are supported?

Linear and GitHub Issues, plus any HTTPS endpoint of yours. A site with no tracker keeps its notes in the workspace. Jira, GitLab, Trello and Notion are not available yet.

Where is my data stored?

In your tracker, or in the PostgreSQL of the instance: in Europe for the Cloud, on your server when you host it. Tracker keys are stored encrypted. What the widget collects lists every field.

Does it slow the site down?

The script loads with defer, after the page is parsed, and draws in its own shadow root. The build fails if it grows past 150 kB gzipped.
